# Stop answering questions. Start answering decisions

Something came up to my mind suddenly: why do so many of us think only at the surface level?

I've noticed it everywhere — When given a task, most people execute it exactly as stated, without ever asking *why* the task exists. The task gets done, but the problem behind it doesn't get solved.

Let me show you what I mean with two stories.

## Scenario A: The Client Meeting

A manager is hosting an important client and asks two employees, on separate occasions, to grab coffee for the meeting.

**Person A** goes straight to the café and buys two coffees. Only after handing them over does he learn the client drinks only iced coffee, no sugar. The coffee sits untouched. A small thing — but small things set the tone of a meeting.

**Person B** pauses for ten seconds and asks: "Does the client have a preference?" He comes back with exactly what the client wanted. It didn't sign the deal by itself — deals aren't won over coffee — but it signaled something to the client: *this team pays attention to details.* And in business, attentiveness compounds.

Same task. Same effort, more or less. Completely different outcome — because one person answered the request, and the other answered the *purpose* behind it.

## Scenario B: The Procurement Task

A coffee shop owner is quietly planning a new product line. He needs new overseas suppliers, but he doesn't share the plan. He simply tells two employees: "Survey the price of Arabica coffee beans."

**Person A** does exactly that. He collects prices and presents a tidy list. Task complete. But the boss can't make any decision from it — a price list without context answers nothing. Now the boss has to issue another instruction, then another. Person A becomes an execution machine that requires detailed commands for every step.

**Person B** starts from a different question: *why would the boss suddenly care about Arabica prices?* He figures there's probably a new product or supplier change coming. So he surveys the market properly: wholesale versus small-order pricing, procurement cost, logistics cost, lead times, the trade-offs of each supplier, and how the numbers look short-term versus long-term. He presents not a price list, but a decision-ready picture.

You could argue the boss should have shared his plans, but here's the uncomfortable truth: context is withheld all the time in life and businesses, deliberately or not.

The people who investigate the intent anyway are the ones who can solve bigger problems.

> Do you think that Elon Musk will hire person A?

## The Underlying Logic

The difference between Person A and Person B is a habit. It's the question they ask themselves before doing something:

- Person A asks: **"What was I told to do?"**
- Person B asks: **"what is the underlying logic beneath the surface"**

Every task you're given is downstream of a decision someone is trying to make. When you only answer the question as literally stated, you return raw material. When you answer the decision, you return value.

This meme literally resonates 😂: *"If you're homeless, just buy a house."* 

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=US4l0V08UHk

You don't need any framework to practice it. We need to spend one minute asking *"why"*. A small difference in how you think can open a wide gap between two people in the long run. Imagine losing a $10 million deal because your competitor asked one better question.

## The Takeaway

> Like everybody reading this article, we all have choices, and the choices that we make every day can reshape, remold, and redefine the future. - Michael Crossland

**Stop answering questions. Start answering decisions.**
